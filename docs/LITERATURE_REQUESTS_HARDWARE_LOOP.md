# Literature requests — the hardware loop

> **Acquisition review, September 6:** the [reconciled literature audit](audit_work/LREKIT_LITERATURE_AUDIT_2026-09-05.md)
> is the current source-status and applicability index. It identifies already-held
> but unindexed material, corrects NASA identifiers, and distinguishes historical
> access restrictions from verified current availability. Scope-dependent process,
> trim and test recommendations below remain subject to that review.

Numbering continues the existing ledgers rather than restarting: items **1–26** are
in `LITERATURE_REQUESTS.md`, **G1–G14** in `LITERATURE_REQUESTS_GENERALIZATION.md`.
This document adds **H1–H14**.

Every request below was generated by hitting a specific wall while reading
`propulsion_texts/` this session, not by listing what a turbopump program
conventionally owns. Each row says what the document must *contain*, so a
substitute can be judged.

## 0. The headline finding, which reduces this list

**Manufacturing is the least literature-blocked of the three hardware tracks.**
NASA SP-8109 §2.3.3 and §3.3.3 — already on disk at
`propulsion_texts/fuel_pump_design/19740020848.pdf`, and verified against the scan
this session — constitute a quantitative, part-specific process model for a
centrifugal impeller: process selection keyed to tip speed, the machinable blade
number `Z₂ ≤ ≈ 28 sin β₂`, cutter length-to-diameter limits of 10 (aluminium) and 8
(titanium), achievable cast surface finishes by part diameter, the spin-test
requirement above 1000 fps, and a residual-imbalance formula. The Gradl series
(`gradl2016`–`gradl2020.pdf`) supplies the additive-manufacturing counterpart.

What is genuinely blocked is the **mechanical** side. SP-8109 repeatedly states a
criterion and defers the data to a document we do not hold. Three of those deferrals
are already requests 13–15 in the existing ledger.

**SP-8109's reference list was rendered and read off the scan during this session**
(printed p. 102, PDF p. 114), which converted most of the remaining "find something
about X" requests into named documents with report numbers — and several of them are
NACA or NASA reports that are probably free. That changes the acquisition order
enough that §6 leads with a single downloadable batch. The one thing the exercise
established as **unobtainable** is equally useful to know: SP-8109's impeller
mechanical-design references are Confidential, so its structural method is not
recoverable from its own bibliography and a substitute must be used (H1c).

## 2. New requests — Tier 1, blocking a track

### H1 — Pressure distribution on rotating non-pumping surfaces *(the axial-thrust integrand)*

**Blocks:** Track J1 outright. Without it the axial force ledger cannot close, which
means the wear-ring diameters cannot be sized, which means the residual load on the
bearing is unknown, which means the bearing cannot be selected even once item 13
arrives.

SP-8109 §2.5.1 names the deferral precisely:

> "In the pump, pressure gradients occur on the smooth nonpumping impeller hub and
> shroud surfaces as well as on the open faces of impellers. The pressure gradients
> on the nonpumping surfaces caused by viscous forces may be calculated by the
> procedures presented in **reference 79**. The pressures on the face of an open
> impeller may be calculated by procedures presented in **references 53, 54, and 80**."

**The references are now resolved.** SP-8109's reference list was rendered and read
off the scan this session (printed p. 102, PDF p. 114):

| SP-8109 ref | Full citation | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| **79** | **Due, H. F. Jr.**, *An Empirical Method for Calculating Radial Pressure Distribution on Rotating Discs*, J. Eng. Power, Trans. ASME, Series A, **vol. 88, 1966, pp. 188–196** | **This is the one to get first.** ASME journal — resolvable by DOI |
| **80** | **Young, W. E.; and Due, H. F.**, *Investigation of Pressure Prediction Methods for Low-Flow Radial Impellers, Phase III*, **PWA FR-1761**, Pratt & Whitney Aircraft, United Aircraft Corp. (West Palm Beach, FL), Jan. 13, 1966 | contractor report; try DTIC/NTRS |
| **87** | Young, W. E., et al., *…Phase II*, **UAC-PFR-1276**, United Aircraft Corp. (E. Hartford, CT), Mar. 1965 | the Phase II companion of ref. 80 |
| **53** | **Katsanis, T.**, *Use of Arbitrary Quasi-orthogonals for Calculating Flow Distribution in the Meridional Plane of a Turbomachine*, **NASA TN D-2546**, 1964 | **NTRS, free** |
| **54** | **Katsanis, T.; and McNally, W. D.**, *Fortran Program for Calculating Velocities and Streamlines on a Blade-to-Blade Stream Surface of a Tandem Blade Turbomachine*, **NASA TN D-5044**, 1969 | **NTRS, free** |

**Must contain (ref. 79):** the radial pressure distribution in a rotating
fluid-filled cavity between an impeller shroud and a stationary housing — the
rotating-core factor (commonly quoted as ≈ ½ ω for a closed cavity, but the point is
to source it rather than assume it), and its dependence on cavity aspect ratio,
surface roughness, through-flow (leakage radially inward or outward), and Reynolds
number.

**Bonus:** refs. 53 and 54 are free NASA TNs and are *also* the meridional and
blade-to-blade flow-analysis methods SP-8109 §2.3.1 alludes to when it says
"quasi-three-dimensional analyses are used to estimate more accurately the velocity
distributions within the impeller." They are therefore relevant to Track H1's choice
of β distribution as well as to the thrust balance.

### H1b — Self-compensating thrust balance system performance

**Blocks:** Track J1's balance-piston and hydrostatic-bearing branch — the branch
SP-8109 §2.5.2.3 says is *required* when ball bearings cannot take the residual
axial load, which is the regime an electric pump at 30–60 krpm sits in.

Resolved from the same rendered reference page:

> **81. Watters, W. E.; and Luehr, L.**: *Development of Steady-State and Dynamic
> Performance Prediction Methods for Turbopump Self-Compensating Thrust Balance
> Systems*. **NASA CR-72630**, Aerojet-General Corp. (Sacramento, CA), Mar. 1970.

A NASA contractor report, so **likely free on NTRS**. Must contain: balance-piston
and hydrostatic-bearing sizing, the self-compensating clearance behaviour, and the
**effective axial spring rate** — which SP-8109 says must be sufficient "to avoid
axial resonances of the rotating assembly", making it a rotordynamic input as well
as a force-balance one.

### H1c — Impeller structural design: a substitute is required, not the original

**Status: the original is unobtainable, and this should be recorded rather than left
as an open request.**

SP-8109 §3.3.2.4 says "Impeller disk stresses and deflections should be calculated
by procedures similar to those presented in references 61, 82, and 83." Refs. 82 and
83 are, verbatim from the rendered reference page:

> **82.** *Mechanical Design Aspects of Centrifugal Impellers with High Tip Speed
> Capability (U)*. Rep. 7446-01F (AD 350716), Aerojet-General Corp., Mar. 1963.
> **(Confidential).**
>
> **83.** *Mechanical Design Aspects of Centrifugal Impellers with High Tip Speed
> Capability: The Results of Experimental Program (U)*. Phase II Final Rep. 7446-01F
> (AD 340146), Aerojet-General Corp., Aug. 1963. **(Confidential).**

So SP-8109's impeller structural method **is not recoverable from its own
references**. What remains usable and is already extracted: eq. (22) for preliminary
blade thickness, the 30 %-of-steady cyclic loading rule, the modified Goodman
criterion (fig. 23), the hub-to-tip ratio trade, and the monograph's own statement
that spin tests with stress coat and strain gauges are *required* because stress
concentrations are difficult to analyse.

**Substitute needed:** a rotating-disc stress reference (disc and hub stress under
centrifugal load, with blade-root and shroud-attachment treatment) plus, for the
allowables, **ref. 78 — *Aerospace Structural Metals Handbook*, AFML-TR-68-115,
vols. 1–3, Air Force Materials Laboratory (WPAFB, OH), 1969** — which SP-8109 cites
directly and which is a general, obtainable allowables source that would also serve
`raosim/materials.py`.

### H2 — Volute area law with friction correction

**Blocks:** the accuracy, not the existence, of Track H5.

SP-8109 §2.4.1.3.1: the constant-moment-of-momentum method "**corrected for friction
losses (ref. 74)**… was used in the design of the J-2S Mark 29 fuel pump, which
experienced very light radial bearing loads."

Resolved:

> **74. Brown, W. B.; and Bradshaw, G. R.**: *Design and Performance of Family of
> Diffusing Scrolls with Mixed-Flow Impeller and Vaneless Diffuser*. **NACA Report
> 936, 1949.**

A NACA report — **freely available**, and the highest ratio of value to acquisition
effort in this entire list. Companion worth taking at the same time: **ref. 75,
Knapp, R. T., *Centrifugal-Pump Performance as Affected by Design Features*, Trans.
ASME vol. 63, 1941, pp. 251–260**, which is the source of SP-8109's fig. 29
(impeller discharge pressure vs angular distance from the tongue, for three volute
types across a flow range) — i.e. the off-design radial-load data.

**Must contain:** the friction correction to the r·c_u = const area distribution, and
ideally the resulting radial-load prediction. The **uncorrected** law is
implementable today from §2.4.1.3.1 alone, so this is an accuracy request rather
than a blocker — but the radial load it predicts is what Track J sizes a bearing
against, so the correction has real consequences downstream.

### H2b — Low-thrust pump design and testing *(directly relevant to Track L)*

Found while resolving the reference list, and it addresses the 500 N problem head-on:

> **55.** *Rotating and Positive-Displacement Pumps for Low-Thrust Rocket Engines.*
> **Vol. 1 — Evaluation and Design; Vol. 2 — Fabrication and Testing. NASA CR-72965
> and CR-72966**, Rocketdyne Div., Rockwell International.

NASA contractor reports, so **likely free on NTRS**. This is a study of pumps for
*low-thrust* engines, covering both design and fabrication/testing, from the same
organisation and era as SP-8109. If it contains what its title suggests, it is the
single most relevant document in existence for the question "where does the
centrifugal meanline stop being the right answer as thrust falls, and what replaces
it" — which is exactly Track L's envelope question and exactly the regime where the
current stack crashes.

Note the title's phrasing: **"Rotating *and positive-displacement*"**. That the
study considered positive-displacement pumps at all is itself the answer to part of
the question.

### H3 — Pump performance test standard

**Blocks:** Track K1's acceptance conventions and K2's adjudication.

**Nothing in `propulsion_texts/` defines a test *procedure*.** SP-8109 gives
*conventions* — its fig. 4 data are at **2 % head loss for pumps and 10 % for
inducers**, notably *not* the commercial 3 % — but not how to run the test, how many
points, at what stabilisation criteria, or how to reduce the data.

**Candidates:** **ISO 9906** *Rotodynamic pumps — Hydraulic performance acceptance
tests*, or **ANSI/HI 14.6**, or **ASME PTC 8.2** *Centrifugal Pumps*.

**Must contain:** the H–Q–η–NPSH test procedure, required instrumentation and its
accuracy classes, stabilisation and repeatability criteria, the reduction equations
from raw channels to reported quantities, acceptance grades and tolerance bands, and
the NPSH test convention with its head-drop criterion stated explicitly.

**Which schema field it fills:** `acceptance_convention`, `reduction_equation`, and
the instrument accuracy class that feeds `uncertainty`.

---

### H4 — Measurement uncertainty framework

**Blocks:** the `uncertainty` field of the measurement record meaning anything, and
therefore K2's ability to say whether a residual is significant.

**Candidates:** **ISO/IEC Guide 98-3 (GUM)**, *Guide to the Expression of
Uncertainty in Measurement*; or **ASME PTC 19.1** *Test Uncertainty*.

**Must contain:** Type A vs Type B evaluation, combined standard uncertainty,
coverage factors and expanded uncertainty, propagation through a data-reduction
equation, and correlated-input treatment. The last is not optional here: c\* is
computed from P_c, A_t and ṁ, whose errors are not independent.

**Note:** Reis 2019 — the most complete pump test in the corpus — quotes **no
uncertainty at all**. That is the norm in this literature and it is precisely why the
schema must demand it rather than inherit the habit.

---

### H5 — Flow measurement standard

**Blocks:** the largest single uncertainty term in any pump or injector test.

**Candidate:** **ISO 5167** (parts 1–4), *Measurement of fluid flow by means of
pressure differential devices inserted in circular cross-section conduits*.

**Must contain:** orifice, nozzle and venturi discharge coefficients with their
uncertainty, installation and straight-length requirements, expansibility, and the
Reynolds-number range of validity. Reis 2019 uses "flowmeters" and a "calibrated
orifice" with neither coefficient nor uncertainty stated.

## 3. New requests — Tier 2, needed to certify rather than to build

### H6 — Achievable tolerance and fits by process

**Candidates:** **ISO 2768** (general tolerances), **ISO 286** (ISO system of limits
and fits). Optionally a machining-practice handbook for achievable-tolerance-by-
process tables.

**Why:** ASME Y14.5-2018 is already in the corpus and defines the *language* of
tolerance — datums, feature control frames, material conditions. It does not say
what a process can *achieve*. Track I1's `achievable_tolerance` field and Track J5's
cold-vs-operating fit statements both need the values, and `COMPLETION_PLAN.md` G5
is currently blocked partly on this.

### H7 — Bolted joint preload and a selected fastener family

**Candidate:** **VDI 2230** *Systematic calculation of highly stressed bolted
joints*, plus a selected NAS/MS/ISO fastener standard.

**Why:** already flagged in `COMPLETION_PLAN.md` §10 as "partial — request fastener
selection/standards". SP-8109 §2.4.3.2 covers fasteners and attachments at the
practice level; the preload calculation and the fastener catalogue are elsewhere.
Track J6 and the split-casing work both need it.

### H8 — Turbomachinery blade geometry and flank milling

**Candidate:** a turbomachinery blade-design reference covering ruled-surface blade
definition and 5-axis flank milling geometry — for example a text on the geometric
definition of ruled blade surfaces and cutter-path generation, or a CAM-side
treatment of flank-milling error for ruled and near-ruled surfaces.

**Why:** Track H3 must decide whether a generated blade is flank-millable or must be
point-milled, and Track I2 must check tool reachability against the answer. SP-8109
constrains this from the outside (`Z₂ ≤ 28 sin β₂`, cutter L/D ≤ 10 / 8) but never
defines the surface geometry. `propulsion_texts/L-05_BSplines_NURBS.pdf` and
`CAD_04.pdf` cover representation, not machinability.

### H9 — Inverse / prescribed-loading blade design

**Candidate:** a treatment of centrifugal impeller blade design by prescribed blade
loading or prescribed velocity distribution.

**Why:** Track H1 must choose a β(u) distribution. SP-8109 says only that the
distribution "and the blade number are adjusted until desired design limits are
achieved", with velocity gradients checked for eddies and separation. That is a
criterion without a method. The default (linear in r, as now) is defensible as a
starting point; anything better needs a source.

### H10 — Rotordynamics of high-speed pumps

**Candidate:** a rotordynamics text covering critical-speed calculation
(transfer-matrix or finite-element), bearing and seal stiffness and damping
coefficients, and subsynchronous whirl stability.

**Why:** SP-8101 (item 14) may supply the turbopump-specific method, but the
Lomakin effect — the rotordynamic stiffness of an annular seal or wear ring, which
is a *design variable the toolkit controls* through clearance — needs a general
treatment. SP-8109 §2.2.1.1 warns about subsynchronous whirl instability above the
first critical without giving a stability criterion.

### H11 — Cryogenic and propellant-lubricated bearing practice

**Why:** SP-8048 (item 13) is from 1971. If the toolkit is to serve LOX and methane
electric-pump designs at modern speeds, a current source on propellant-lubricated
and hybrid-ceramic bearings would materially change the design space. Lower priority
than item 13 itself — get the monograph first, then judge whether this is needed.

### H13 — SP-8109 equations (20) and (21), rendered

The diffuser-vane / impeller-blade **wave reinforcement** criterion. The prose is
clear — reinforcement occurs when the index m is an integer, over Z₂, Z_d, πD_v, the
speed of sound a, and the mean relative velocity W — but both equations are
OCR-scrambled. Track H5 needs them before coding the vane-count gate.

Same for **equation (22)**, the preliminary blade thickness
`t ≈ 0.087 b_t2 U_t2 / (sin β_t2 σ_allow)`: the constant and the form should be seen
before being implemented, since it would replace a geometric ratio with a
structural one.

### H14 — A rocket-engine test instrumentation reference

**Why:** Huzel & Huang SP-125 covers engine testing and performance measurement and
is already held; this request is for whatever supplements it on modern
instrumentation — thermocouple and heat-flux gauge practice for chamber walls,
high-frequency pressure measurement for combustion stability, and the reduction of
measured wall temperature to heat flux. Perakis 2021 (`perakis2021.pdf`, already
held) does the last of these as an inverse problem and may be sufficient on its own;
read it before requesting more.

## 5. What I checked and concluded we do **not** need

- **A GD&T standard.** ASME Y14.5-2018, Y14.24-2012 and Y14.41-2003 are all in the
  corpus. Y14.41 in particular is the right reference for a model-based, drawing-less
  definition, which is what a STEP-only release is. `COMPLETION_PLAN.md` G5 is *not*
  blocked on GD&T language — only on achievable values (H6) and fastener selection
  (H7).
- **An AM design-rules handbook.** The Gradl series is NASA LRE-specific, which is
  better than a generic handbook: overhang rule, as-built finish, powder removal as a
  design constraint, and the observed failure modes with their fixes.
- **A general pump textbook.** SP-8109 plus White's *Fluid Mechanics* (held) cover
  the meanline adequately for design; the gaps are all in *mechanical* design and
  *test* practice, which is where this list concentrates.
- **A slip-factor source — but with a correction.** SP-8109's *text* does not endorse
  a named slip correlation; it works in head coefficient directly, ψ = f(φ₂, β₂, Z₂),
  which avoids needing one. Its *reference list* does cite one, and it is the obvious
  choice if an explicit slip factor is ever wanted: **ref. 56 — Wiesner, F. J., *A
  Review of Slip Factors for Centrifugal Impellers*, J. Eng. Power, Trans. ASME,
  Series A**. Resolvable by DOI. Matching the monograph's own ψ formulation remains
  the more defensible default; this is the fallback if a slip factor is needed.

- **A LOX materials-compatibility standard, separately.** SP-8109 §3.3.2.6 recommends
  "inert wearing materials such as Teflon, Kel-F, and silver" for oxidizer pumps and
  cites **ref. 84 — MSFC-Spec-106A, *Testing Compatibility of Materials for Liquid
  Oxygen Systems*, Marshall Space Flight Center, Sept. 1966**. Worth acquiring
  alongside the others rather than as its own request, since it is one document and
  it converts a materials recommendation into a test criterion.

## 6. Suggested acquisition order

Resolving SP-8109's reference list changed this order substantially: several items
that looked like open-ended searches turned out to be named NACA/NASA reports that
are probably free.

**Batch 1 — free or near-free, and each unblocks a track.** One session of
downloading.

| Get | What it unblocks |
|---|---|
| **NACA Report 936** (Brown & Bradshaw 1949) — H2 | the friction-corrected volute law → Track H5 → the radial load Track J needs |
| **NASA CR-72965 / CR-72966** — H2b, low-thrust pumps | Track L's envelope question, at the exact thrust class where the stack currently crashes |
| **NASA CR-72630** (Watters & Luehr 1970) — H1b | balance pistons and hydrostatic bearings, i.e. the branch an electric pump at 30–60 krpm actually needs |
| **NASA TN D-2546** and **TN D-5044** (Katsanis) — part of H1 | meridional and blade-to-blade flow analysis → Track H1's β distribution |
| **NASA SP-8048, SP-8101, SP-8121** — existing items 13, 14, 15 | the three monographs SP-8109 defers its mechanical data to; Track J is blocked on all three |

**Batch 2 — no acquisition at all, only rendering work.** H12 and H13. This yields
seven flight-hardware benchmark points from fig. 13, a ~20-pump geometry table
(Table I), the efficiency-surrogate upgrade from fig. 5, and the two equations Track
H5 needs before coding. Method and page offset are recorded in H12. **Do this batch
first if download access is not immediately available — it needs nothing but the
files already on disk.**

**Batch 3 — paid or institutional.**
`Due 1966` (ASME, H1's primary), `Wiesner` (ASME, if a slip factor is wanted), then
**H3** (ISO 9906 / HI 14.6 / ASME PTC 8.2), **H4** (GUM or ASME PTC 19.1) and **H5**
(ISO 5167). These three are needed *before* the first measurement is ingested, not
after — a datapoint recorded without a defined acceptance convention or an
uncertainty statement cannot be retrofitted with one.

**Batch 4 — to certify rather than to build.**
**H6** (ISO 2768, ISO 286), **H7** (VDI 2230 + a fastener family), and the
*Aerospace Structural Metals Handbook* (AFML-TR-68-115) from H1c, which also serves
`raosim/materials.py`. Plus MSFC-Spec-106A for LOX compatibility.

**Batch 5 — as the tracks reach them.** H8–H11, H14.

**Recorded as unobtainable, not outstanding:** SP-8109 refs. 82 and 83, the impeller
mechanical-design reports, are **Confidential** (AD 350716, AD 340146). Track J4
must proceed on eq. (22), the Goodman criterion, and a substitute rotating-disc
stress reference — and must carry the monograph's own admission that spin testing,
not analysis, is what establishes local stress at concentrations.
